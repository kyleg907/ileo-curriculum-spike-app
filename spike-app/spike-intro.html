Introduction to the LEGO® Education SPIKE™ Robot

Your robot kit contains

  • a SPIKE intelligent brick (the programmable brain of the robot)
  • motors, sensors, custom wires to connect to the brick
  • a rechargeable battery
  • a USB cable to connect a computer and to charge the robot’s battery,
  • LEGO® TECHNIC™ parts to construct the body and attachments for the robot. Normally we use LEGO MINDSTORMS® Education kits purchased directly from the LEGO company, but if your school purchased the retail kits elsewhere you might notice some differences in the available parts and sensors. The rest of this document will assume you have the SPIKE Prime kit.

You might see, or even use, LEGO MINDSTORMS EV3 and LEGO MINDSTORMS NXT robots in your classroom. Other documents discuss those robots and programming software.

The SPIKE™ Intelligent brick (aka the ‘brick’)

Let’s look at the SPIKE brick briefly. The brick contains a micro USB port and four ports to connect to motors labeled A through F. Typically port B connects to the left motor, and port C connects to the right motor. The SPIKE brick has a USB Micro-B connector which we use to connect to a Windows, Mac, or ChromeBook (with the SPIKE web app) computer to download programs to the robot. The SPIKE also supports Bluetooth for downloading programs to the robot.

Motors

SPIKE kits contain one large motor and two medium motors. SPIKE robots typically use the medium motors to move the robot, and the large motor to drive accessories.

Caution

Avoid pushing the robot across the floor/table or spinning the motor quickly with your hands: that can DAMAGE your motor! Spinning the motors slowly is ok: some programs might use the motor position as a way to affect the behavior of the robot.

When we program the motor to go clockwise (the default) with the blue motor blocks, the motor drum will turn clockwise as in Figure 2. With the pink movement blocks, forward means “turn the motor clockwise”. You’ll learn more about programming movement in the next lesson.

The Driving Base 1 robot uses medium motors to drive the robot, and the large motor for accessories.

SPIKE™ App software

To make the robot do what you want, we’ll use the SPIKE App to create a program and then download and run it on the robot. SPIKE App runs on devices including Windows and Mac computers, iPad, Android tablets and Chromebook. Follow your instructor’s guidance on what platform to use as schools or organizations have different policies and access to these devices and software.

Launch SPIKE App from the Start Menu, desktop, Finder, etc. depending on what platform you use. The program will start and show the home screen. SPIKE App appears to download information when first loaded and you might notice a delay starting the program the first time on some platforms.

On the Select your SPIKE™ solution screen, click SPIKE Prime (the bike rider robot on the right hand side).

This will take you to the SPIKE Prime home screen.

Tge blocks in the SPIKE App programming environment on your or tablet make up the instructions to your robot. Each of the blocks is a different action or function that the robot can perform. By combining these blocks, you can write out the steps for your plan in a way the robot can follow. This sequence of blocks is your robot’s “program.”

We’ll grab our first program block from the Block Palette and connect it to the Start block and program the robot to drive forward later in this lesson.

The SPIKE web app

As of this writing, the LEGO group offers a web-based portal for programming SPIKE robots at https://spike.legoeducation.com. If your device is Windows-based or a Chromebook and the device has Google’s Chrome web browser, this app lets you program your SPIKE robot without having to install an app. You must use the USB cable to download programs to the robot.

Follow your instructor’s guidance on whether you’ll be using an app on your device, or the web version.

Caution

With the SPIKE web app, your projects are not saved automatically. Most of the apps you use probably save data as you work. Do not lose the program you’ve worked so hard on!

You must remember to download your project by clicking the ‘Save’ button to download a file to your computer. Be clear on what directory or cloud folder (in the case of Chromebooks) you’ve put it.

In the next class, you can open the SPIKE App, and open this file from your computer so you can resume your work.

Build instructions

SPIKE™ App contains the instructions for the Driving Base (and other) robots. The plans are also available online at https://ibm.biz/BdP2us. For this first lesson we only need a basic driving robot, known as the Driving Base 1: we’ll add sensors add attachments in later lessons. Your instructor might provide other plans today as an alternative.

  1. Click Home to go to the home screen.

  2. Scroll down and click the Unit Plans area.

  3. On the next screen, check if the Competition Ready plan needs to be downloaded. If it appears with a DOWNLOAD prompt as in Figure 1.2 (a), click it once to download the plans. While we will not use this lesson plan, it will download robot build instructions we’ll need.

  4. Once the download has completed, click Home to return to the home screen.

  5. From the home screen, scroll down to Building Instructions

  6. Click Building Instructions to show the available plans. Of this screen does not show instructions, the download for the Competition ready lesson has not completed.

  7. Find and open Driving Base 1. You’ll see the following page where the first step is to connect the SPIKE battery.

Follow the rest of the instructions and build the robot now. Do not build any accessories for the robot or follow any other build instructions in the SPIKE App, unless directed by the lessons. Proceed to the next section after you build your robot.
